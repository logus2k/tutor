# Manage Uploaded Knowledge and Connectors (e.g., Google Drive, Gmail)

Claude can draw on two kinds of external knowledge: **files you upload** and **connectors** that link Claude to live services like Google Drive, Gmail, and Google Calendar. This topic tests your judgment about *what to connect, what permissions that grants, how the data is accessed, and how to keep sources relevant* — the everyday governance a business user is responsible for.

## Two ways to give Claude your knowledge

**Uploaded files** are static snapshots. You attach a document to a single chat or, more durably, to a Project's knowledge base, and Claude reads that copy. If the underlying document changes later, the uploaded copy does not — you must re-upload.

**Connectors** link Claude to a live source, so Claude reads the current state on demand. The Google Workspace connectors (Google Drive, Gmail, Google Calendar) are the ones the exam names explicitly. Google Docs added through the Drive connector "sync directly from Google Drive, so you're always working with the latest version" — a key advantage over a stale uploaded copy.

Choosing between them is a judgment call: upload a fixed reference (a signed contract, a finalized policy) so it cannot drift; connect a living source (an evolving planning doc, your inbox, your calendar) so Claude always sees current reality.

## Managing uploaded files

Files can be added to an individual chat or to a Project's **Files** section for persistent reuse. Limits differ by context:

- **Per chat:** up to 500 MB per file and up to 20 files per conversation.
- **Per project:** 30 MB per file, unlimited count, but the total must fit within Claude's working capacity (RAG mode kicks in on paid plans as knowledge approaches the context limit).

Supported types include PDF, DOCX, CSV, TXT, HTML, XLSX, and common image formats. For most non-PDF files Claude extracts **text only** — images embedded inside a Word or slide file are not analyzed — so a scanned, image-only PDF may yield little. Keeping sources relevant means uploading clean, current, well-labeled documents and removing superseded ones, rather than dumping every version into the knowledge base and hoping Claude picks the right one.

## Adding and using connectors

Google Workspace connectors are available to all Claude users and on Claude Desktop. Setup requires you to **authenticate directly with your Google account** through Google's OAuth consent screen. On Team and Enterprise plans, connectors must first be enabled at the organization level by an Owner or Primary Owner before individual members can connect — an intentional governance gate.

Once connected, you don't manually route requests. When you ask a question that needs your mail, calendar, or Drive, Claude automatically detects which tool it needs and uses it. Files can also be pulled in deliberately: click the **+**, choose **Add from Google Drive**, and search recent files or paste a document URL. (Note: the Drive connector operates in the Files section of **private** projects; shared projects disable it.)

## Permissions, scope, and approval — the part the exam cares about

Several safeguards define what a connector can and cannot do, and they are exactly the kind of detail an exam question probes:

- **Claude mirrors your existing permissions.** You cannot use Claude to reach anything you couldn't already open yourself in Google Workspace. A connector does not escalate access.
- **Explicit approval per action.** Each action Claude takes on your behalf requires your approval. Claude does not silently rummage through your account.
- **Gmail is read-and-draft only — never send.** Claude only reads emails and creates drafts with your approval; the send function is not enabled, so every message must be sent manually from your own Gmail. During OAuth, Google's screen may mention send permissions, but Claude's behavior is restricted to reading and drafting. A tempting-but-wrong exam answer is "Claude can send the email for you" — it cannot; you send it.
- **Minimum necessary data.** Claude accesses your data only when a specific request requires it and retrieves the least it needs, rather than ingesting your whole account.
- **Citations.** Responses include citations showing which emails, events, or documents were used, with links to the originals where available — so you can verify the basis of an answer.

## Data-access considerations and privacy discipline

Connector data is encrypted at rest and in transit, and information retrieved through a connector is retained alongside the associated chat, so deleting the chat removes it. Even so, connecting a source is a meaningful data decision, not a convenience toggle:

- **Connect deliberately.** A connector exposes real, current business data to the assistant. Connect the sources a workflow genuinely needs and no more.
- **Apply sensitivity controls before, not after.** If regulated personal data lives in the source, the control is redaction, scoping, or simply not connecting it — not asking Claude to ignore or not retain it. "Tell Claude not to keep it" does not satisfy a compliance obligation.
- **Verify connector-grounded claims.** Grounding in your real Drive or inbox reduces but does not remove hallucination risk; Claude can still misread or over-summarize. For high-stakes output, check the cited source.

## Keeping sources relevant over time

Both uploaded files and connectors decay in usefulness if left unmanaged:

- **Remove stale uploads.** When a policy is revised or a contract superseded, delete the old file from Project knowledge and add the new one. Leaving both invites Claude to answer from the outdated version.
- **Review connector scope periodically.** Connections granted for one project may outlive their need. You can toggle individual connectors via the **+ > Connectors** menu, and disconnect entirely under **Customize > Connectors** by selecting the connector, clicking **Disconnect**, and confirming.
- **Prefer live sync for living documents.** If a document changes constantly, a connector's automatic sync beats a manual re-upload cycle that people forget to perform.

## Common pitfalls

- Assuming a connector grants Claude *more* access than you have — it never does; it mirrors your permissions.
- Expecting Claude to send email or take unattended actions — actions need your explicit approval, and Gmail send is off.
- Treating "I uploaded the file once" as permanently current — uploads are snapshots and go stale; connectors sync, files do not.
