# Differentiate Between Claude Model Types (Haiku, Sonnet, Opus)

Anthropic offers Claude in a family of model tiers, not a single model. The exam expects you to know the three headline tiers — **Haiku, Sonnet, and Opus** — and, more importantly, what each is *for*. The names are consistent across generations: a tier tells you the tradeoff between speed and cost on one side and raw capability on the other. Knowing the tier framing lets you pick sensibly without memorizing version numbers.

## The core idea: a speed/cost vs. capability spectrum

Picture a straight line. On one end sits **Haiku** — the fastest and cheapest. On the other end sits **Opus** — the most capable and the slowest and most expensive. **Sonnet** sits in the middle as the balanced default. Every model in the family lands somewhere on that spectrum, and moving toward more capability costs you speed and money, while moving toward more speed and lower cost costs you some depth of reasoning.

The important exam insight is that "more capable" is not universally "better." A model that is overkill for the task wastes latency and budget; a model that is underpowered produces work you cannot use. The right answer is the tier that *matches the task*, which is why the exam frames model choice as a tradeoff rather than a ranking.

## Haiku — fastest and most cost-effective

Haiku is built for speed and volume. It is the tier you reach for when you have many simple operations to perform quickly and cheaply, and when latency matters because a person or a process is waiting on each result.

Best at:
- Classification and routing (tagging tickets, sorting messages by topic).
- Short, structured replies and quick lookups.
- High-volume, repetitive tasks where cost per item adds up fast.
- Latency-sensitive interactions where a snappy response beats a slightly better one.

The tradeoff: Haiku is not the tier for deep, multi-step reasoning or nuanced long-form work. Ask it to untangle a complex analysis and you may get a fast but shallow answer. Current version: **Claude Haiku 4.5**.

## Sonnet — the balanced workhorse

Sonnet is the sensible default for most work. It delivers strong quality at a moderate cost and speed, sitting between Haiku's thrift and Opus's depth. For the great majority of everyday and production tasks, Sonnet is capable enough without paying Opus prices or waiting for Opus latency.

Best at:
- General knowledge work: drafting, summarizing, editing, analysis of moderate complexity.
- Most production workflows where quality matters but the reasoning is not at the frontier of difficulty.
- Being the starting point when you are unsure which tier to use — begin with Sonnet, then move up to Opus only if the output is not good enough, or down to Haiku if speed and cost matter more than the extra quality.

The tradeoff: Sonnet is slower and pricier than Haiku, so for trivial high-volume work it can be more than you need; and for the genuinely hardest problems, Opus may still be worth the step up. Current version: **Claude Sonnet 5**.

## Opus — most capable, for the hardest work

Opus is the most capable tier in the Haiku/Sonnet/Opus family. It is the choice for the hardest reasoning, long-horizon planning, and complex agentic work where several steps chain together and a mistake early on derails everything after. It is also the slowest and most expensive tier, so its value shows up only when the task truly demands that extra capability.

Best at:
- Deep, multi-step reasoning and complex problem-solving.
- Long-horizon, multi-stage tasks and sophisticated agentic workflows.
- High-stakes work where the quality of the final answer justifies the extra time and cost.

The tradeoff: because it is slow and costly, running Opus on simple, high-volume tasks is wasteful — you pay a premium and wait longer for no benefit the task can use. Current version: **Claude Opus 4.8**.

## A note on the wider family

Anthropic also offers **Fable 5** as an even more capable frontier model that sits above Opus. It is worth knowing it exists, but the exam blueprint frames model selection around the three named tiers — Haiku, Sonnet, and Opus — so anchor your thinking on those three. When a question asks you to choose among model *types*, it is testing the Haiku/Sonnet/Opus tradeoff.

## Putting it together: a quick mental table

- **Simple, fast, high-volume, cheap** → Haiku.
- **Balanced, general-purpose, most everyday and production work** → Sonnet (the default).
- **Hardest reasoning, complex/long-horizon, quality over cost** → Opus.

## Common pitfalls the exam tests

- **Treating Opus as the "right" answer by default.** "Use the most capable model" is a tempting but usually wrong choice. For simple, high-volume tasks it wastes cost and latency; the correct answer matches a cheaper, faster tier to the work.
- **Fixating on version numbers.** The exam tests the tier concept, not whether you memorized "4.5" or "5." Know that each tier advances over generations, but reason about the tradeoff.
- **Forgetting latency as a factor.** Cost is not the only reason to prefer Haiku or Sonnet — when a user is waiting on each response, a faster tier can be the better experience even before you consider price.
- **Assuming a bigger model fixes a prompt problem.** If output quality is poor because the instructions are vague, escalating to Opus may not help; a clearer prompt on Sonnet often does. Match the tier to the task's genuine difficulty, not to frustration.
